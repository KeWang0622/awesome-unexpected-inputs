# Research and curation method

[Collection](../README.md) · [Related work](landscape.md) · [Testing](testing.md)

## Scope

A person must produce a documented input that a concrete interface maps to a useful action. We include practical access, creative work and play. We exclude output-only effects, generic automation, ordinary peripherals without a distinct mapping, and papers with no actionable integration. Research and archived examples remain visibly labeled.

“Unexpected” means an alternative to the familiar keyboard/mouse workflow. It is not a judgment about people who use access technology. Software availability does not establish clinical benefit or universal suitability. Voice, non-speech sounds, gaze, head tracking and switch scanning are distinct inputs with different prerequisites.

## What one record means

Every record is an editorially selected project, not necessarily a complete product. Requirements may include commercial software, a separate host, DIY hardware or a paid service. The `released` label means an upstream installer, runnable script, downloadable configuration or ready product exists; it does not mean free, easy, open source, or independently tested. `source-build` covers source/DIY routes, `research` experimental demonstrations, and `archived` legacy material.

Every entry contains an original summary, concrete outcome, device/input, requirements, three brief starting steps, limitations, a primary evidence URL and a source-review date. An evidence note explains what the source supports. A separate source-code URL is included only when useful and verified. Optional demos are linked with provenance, not silently copied.

## Research sequence

1. Search exact and related topic names across GitHub and the web; inspect neighboring lists and specialist directories.
2. Follow promising projects to maintainer documentation, repositories, releases or original research pages.
3. Compare the claim with current installation and compatibility notes. Preserve conflicts or unknowns in the limitations, or leave the project out.
4. Write concise original descriptions and bilingual entry points; do not reuse marketing copy.
5. Check structured records and generated pages, then inspect the public rendering.

The initial research was reviewed on **2026-10-26**, using the maintainer's local date in America/Los_Angeles. Search engines, deleted pages and private repositories limit coverage. A missing search result is not proof that a topic has never been covered. We make no claim of being the first or most complete list, and no prediction of virality.

## Maintenance

New reports enter through issues or pull requests. Review a record's sources before changing its date. A broken URL is a reason to investigate, not automatic evidence that a project is dead; a successful HTTP response does not establish correctness. Keep a legacy entry only if its limitations and historical usefulness are clear. Update [the changelog](../CHANGELOG.md) for meaningful editorial changes.

Counts are generated from the actual records. The machine-readable format makes review and reuse possible without scraping prose. Automated checks validate data and local structure; they do not perform remote installations. See the separate testing protocol before reporting a setup as reproduced.
